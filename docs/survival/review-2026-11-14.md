> status: review, docs only (TASK-430, agent survivaldesign). No code, no game, no sandbox change. The design that answers it: [design-2026-11-14.md](design-2026-11-14.md). One-page summary: [README.md](README.md).

## 0. The answer in ten lines

1. The bot has **no estimate of how strong the hero is** and **no estimate of how dangerous a zone is**. Every rule compares a *proxy* (character level, life percent, a gold amount, a potion count) with a hand-set number. V: 373 knobs in [../knobs.md](../knobs.md), about 150 of them decide safety, supply or zone choice; knobs.md itself says the live effect of an individual knob "is mostly NOT verified".
2. There are **at least nine separate notions of "may the hero be here"** (section 3), each with its own numbers, written by different cards after different incidents. They disagree: the Countess is safe at clvl 10, 11, 13, 14 or 15 depending on which rule you ask (section 3.2).
3. The proxies are wrong exactly where it hurts. V `swap7`: a clvl 12 Paladin died in Black Marsh, monster level 6. The death line says `gap=-6`: every level rule in the tree calls that zone trivial. The real numbers: 22 hostiles at the arrival point, 9 seconds per kill there against 1.5 in Cold Plains, 88 healing potions per 100 kills against 0 (section 4).
4. The rules are **open loop**. Nothing measures what a zone just cost. After the first Black Marsh visit (merc dead after 32 s, town portal at 35 percent life after 77 s) the bot went back three more times in four minutes and died on the fourth entry (V, section 4.2). The only feedback that exists is a ban: binary, time-based, and the cause of the opposite failure, the hero parked in town for 86 percent of 15 hours with 0 deaths (V [../probes/hero14-analysis-2026-11-14.md](../probes/hero14-analysis-2026-11-14.md)).
5. So each incident gets a rule or a number moved, validated against the run that prompted it. The next run fails somewhere the proxy is wrong in the other direction. That is the circle (section 6).
6. What is solid and must stay: the reflex layer (drink, chicken, crowd rule), which gave 0 deaths in four first-hour runs; the logging; the data tables; the simulator (section 7).

## 1. How the decisions are layered today

```
 every guard tick (50 ms)      REFLEX      drink / rejuvenate / town portal / chicken     combat.Decide
 every pack                    TACTICAL    engage, rest first, avoid, retreat, kite        resource.PreEngage, AbortFight, combat crowd/kite
 every plan step               STRATEGIC   which zone, may I enter, when to leave          bot.Plan.Pick + 9 gates + 6 breakers
 every town visit              ECONOMY     what to buy, revive the merc, go back out       town chores, resource.*, merc.Policy
 every quest step              JOURNEY     may the quest start                             task.BossGate, campaign.Rate, route guards
```

The reflex layer reads life and belt and is sound. The trouble is in the strategic, economy and journey layers: they answer "is the hero strong enough for that place" over and over, each with different inputs.

## 3. Conflicts and redundancies

### 3.1 Nine notions of "ready"

Z1 progression row, Z2 plan slice, Z3 gear risk, Z4 kit gate, Z5 recovery, Z6 poverty, Z7 level gap, Z8 readiness model, plus J1 to J5 for quest areas. All are functions of mostly **clvl** and **kit**. None reads the hero's life pool, damage, defence, block or the monsters' real damage. Z8, the one model built to be per threat, is not wired to anything, and its damage, defence and durability rows skip because the bot does not read those values (V docs/readiness.md).

### 3.3 Three and four state machines over one purse

Recovery (Z5), poverty (Z6), kit gate (Z4), trip budget (E2) and the merc revive bar (M1) all watch gold and potions with different lines: recovery starts under 500 gold, poverty under kit cost 190, the trip floor is 60 to 300, the revive needs the quote. V `swap7`: in one eight-minute stretch the log has "recovery: start (gold 70 < kit 500)", "recovery: end (gold 70, reason kit affordable)" 29 s later, "poverty mode: on (gold 70 below kit cost 190)" 53 s after that, "poverty mode: off (kit complete)" 100 s later, and "gear risk: low (kit fine)". Four recoveries, two poverty cycles, four kit-gate trips and one "gear risk: high" in 20 minutes: five subsystems describing one fact (the hero was bleeding gold in a zone it could not afford).

### 3.4 Merc rules pull against each other

`no_merc_add` (Z2) raises slice entries by 2 when the merc is dead; the revive cap (M1) refuses the revive (4 "revive skipped (revive cap)" lines with gold 301, 70, 18, 222 against price 360). Result (V swap7): at clvl 12 without a merc the plan has no open slice ("past every window", 5 times) and falls back to "last eligible", which is Black Marsh or Dark Wood, the zone that had just killed the merc. T4 (leave when the merc is down) logged nothing in three merc deaths.

So today the system is class-blind where it should not be (the plan slices, the gear gate's life table) and hand-tailored where it need not be (seven Andariel rows, of which one has evidence). The binary caster or melee split recurs in five places with five implementations. That is the gap the archetype layer of the design fills (design section 6): what a class contributes to survival is how it is exposed and how it deals damage, both of which can be read from the build and skill tables.

## 6. Diagnosis: why we keep going in circles

1. **Proxy, not quantity.** The real question is physical: can this hero kill this zone's packs before they kill it, and what does that cost in potions and gold. The rules ask about clvl, life percent and gold lines. A proxy is right where it was tuned and wrong one zone, one class or two levels away.
2. **No shared estimate.** Because no component owns "hero power" or "zone danger", each card computed its own slice of it. Nine entry notions, four tables for one boss, five state machines over one purse. Fixing one leaves the others, so the next incident shows the disagreement, and the patch is an exception.
3. **Open loop.** Thresholds are constants. The bot does not learn from the last ten minutes in a zone; the only feedback is a ban. Open-loop rules either flap (four entries in five minutes) or latch (86 percent in town).
4. **Fix by incident.** Each change was validated against the run that caused it. With about 150 interacting knobs, one hour of one class is not a test of any of them; four of eleven breakers logged nothing in 17 hours, so their numbers are untested in both directions.
5. **Guards compose badly.** Each guard is locally reasonable; together they deadlock (ban + route guard + stale target) or cancel (no-merc add + revive cap). Nobody can predict the composition, so it is found live.

The user's reading is right: zone danger is a known quantity (the same monster sets every game, V areamon-trace population runs), character power is the thing that changes, and the bot should react to *that*, not to the clock or the level number.

## 7. What we got right (keep)

- The reflex layer (R1 to R4): 0 deaths in four first-hour runs, and it is a pure function with tests. It stays as the hard invariant under any new system.
- "Warn, never wall" for quests (ADR 0011) and the three bands (too early, ready, comfortable): the right shape; only the input (clvl) is too thin.
- Builds as data (plan tables, combat profiles): the same route for power and danger data.
- The log lines: `safety decision`, `combat fight` (min life, potions, kills, seconds, hostiles left), `minute`, `area summary`, `merc:`. They already carry everything a feedback controller needs; section 4 was computed from them without any new instrumentation.
- The research: areamon-table.csv (population, XP per kill by clvl), boss1-trace (boss stats and an exposure model), monster-attack-trace and hitrec-trace (hit chance, flinch rules), pal-skill-matrix (damage per second by skill). The danger side is mostly known; it is just not used by the bot.
- `tools/levelsim`: a calibrated time model (0.6 and 9.4 percent on two runs) with a beam search; it only lacks a survival term.
- `internal/readiness/areatable.go`: a generator of per-area facts from the install tables at run time with no game data committed. The danger profile generator extends it.
- Shadow and A/B habits (goldab, abmerc): the rollout uses them.
- The death rule and autopsy, corpse recovery, gold stash before risk: independent of zone choice, unchanged.
